# AI policy report

Status: needs-triage

A new report on the AI policy landscape: legislation, regulation and official guidance, ~95% EU, the Netherlands and the US. Other jurisdictions only for major events.

## Decisions (2026-10-13)

- **Scope: the broad AI policy landscape**, not narrowed to dev tooling. The maintainer follows AI policy for work.
- **Jurisdictions:** EU, the Netherlands and the US (federal + states). **EU and NL weigh more than the US**: they come first in the report, and the US is held to the later stage gate below. Elsewhere only when an item scores as a major event.
- **US items are judged by expected impact on the maintainer, their clients and colleagues** (EU/NL-based, not in the US): direct (obligations that reach EU users of US models, clouds or tools; export controls) or indirect (anything that could significantly change the pace or direction of AI development, e.g. frontier-model rules, chip export limits, federal preemption of state laws). A US item that passed its stage gate but has little expected impact stays out.
- **Reader profile** (for Jev's impact question, kept in config): "An AI practitioner based in the Netherlands, advising commercial companies of roughly 500–1,500 employees across many sectors (e.g. retail, insurance, banking), mostly serving the Dutch market. They deploy and integrate AI built by others, largely from US vendors." The sector examples are illustrations only — the profile must not lean on one sector, since clients span many.
- **Name:** ⚖️ "Policy Watch", label `policy`.
- **News feeds skip items older than 7 days**, on every run: news missed during a gap between runs is no longer new and stays missing. Applies to every RSS news source (digital-strategy, EDPB, AP, Rijksoverheid, RDI, news & commentary), not to legislative stages or consultations.
- **Courts: out** for now (candidate second phase, e.g. CourtListener).
- **News and commentary: in, as context**, from authoritative sources only. When none of those sources has anything, the section is empty — no fallback to general news. A day with only news still gets a report; upcoming deadlines alone never make one (they repeat daily).
- **US bills, state and federal:** reported from passing a chamber onward (passed one chamber, passed both, enacted, vetoed), not at introduction or committee report — earlier is too early for US news. Passing a chamber is the early warning for laws that are coming and need preparing for.
- **New API keys are acceptable.** Set as repo secrets on 2026-10-13: `USA_GOV_API_KEY` (api.data.gov: Congress.gov; whether it also covers Regulations.gov is unverified) and `PLURAL_API_KEY` (Open States). No LegiScan key: Plural is the US-state source.

## Proposed design (not yet decided)

- **Daily diff with state.** Policy events are dated (a stage, a publication, a vote), so a daily report doesn't repeat itself as long as a committed state file records what was already reported (like `digests/web-state.json`). Sources without an updated-since filter (Have your say, EP procedures) are snapshotted and compared against that file. Skipped on a day with nothing new.
- **Layout:** Highlights → EU → Netherlands → US federal → US states → Upcoming deadlines → News & commentary → Signals → Worth a Look.
- **Code renders status, stage and dates; the LLM writes the summaries only** (the trending pattern). A wrong legal status is worse than a wrong star count.
- **Upcoming deadlines (next 30 days), rendered in code** from `commentEndDate` (Regulations.gov), `comments_close_on` (Federal Register), `feedbackEndDate` (Have your say), and the watchlist's calendar of application dates (e.g. AI Act milestones).
- **Selection: the watchlist and Jev carry it, not keywords.** The spike measured keyword discovery as mostly noise in the US rule sources (Federal Register: 0 of 6 hits in a week about AI policy; Regulations.gov: 2–3 of 24), and an AI-policy item without an AI keyword (EDPB: the Dutch DPA's €825M Uber fine for automated decision-making). Keywords cast the net; Jev scores each item (about AI policy? binding or not? a new stage or routine? significance 0–2; for US items, expected impact on an EU/NL-based AI practitioner and their clients, direct or indirect, 0–2 — the question needs a short reader profile, kept in config), with verdicts cached like `digests/trending-classifications.json`. Low-volume feeds (EDPB) skip the keyword step and go to Jev whole.
- **Stage gate:** see Decisions; a watchlisted bill is reported whenever it moves. Introductions and referrals stay out (Congress.gov: ~10 AI-titled a week, nearly all referrals).
- **Watchlist in `config.yml`:** the main files that always appear when they move — EU procedures (AI Act and its amendments, the Digital Omnibus on AI, CRA), Federal Register dockets/RINs, federal bills, and AI provisions inside non-AI bills (NDAA, appropriations) that title matching misses. It also holds the deadline calendar.
