## Signals
A clear signal across the submissions is the shift from monolithic prompting to structured, verifiable agent architectures. Works like MetaSkill and Bellman Language Models show that caching decisions or formalizing skill boundaries can dramatically reduce compute while preserving flexibility. Parallel to this, verification‑driven loops (VERAS, Hippo, DrugHarness) are emerging as a generic pattern for self‑correction without model updates, pointing toward a new paradigm of “agentic learning” that relies on external memory and feedback rather than weight changes. Safety and alignment are receiving dedicated benchmarks (AM‑Bench, HardEval, OR‑License) that probe not just outright harmful outputs but subtle misalignment, compliance gaps, and real‑world feasibility, indicating the community’s recognition that agent deployment will hinge on rigorous, domain‑specific evaluation. Finally, domain‑grounded harnesses (MuninAI, DrugHarness, ReTRACE, TCMScience) demonstrate that coupling LLMs with curated tools, literature indices, and experimental feedback loops can yield specialist agents that rival or exceed expert performance, suggesting a future where LLM‑based research assistants become routine in laboratories, clinics, and engineering teams.
